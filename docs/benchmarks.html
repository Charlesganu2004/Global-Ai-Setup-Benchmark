          Findings

          In plain sentences, with their numbers.

          • On the light class and the original questions, one agent using "PGR: graph and search" needed 134k tokens and 2.0 turns for the three lookups, against 294k tokens and 4.0 turns with no index: 54% fewer tokens.
          • 3 of 3 runs were fully correct with "PGR: graph and search" and 2 of 3 with no index. A run is fully correct when all three answers are.
          • Two turns is the floor for this task: one to ask and one to answer. On the original questions "PGR: graph and search" answered in 2 turns on both classes, with 134k tokens on the light class and 133k on the mid class; "Our engine, first generation, with embeddings" also took 2 turns on both, with 134k tokens on the light class and 133k on the mid class.
          • Tokens follow turns. On the light class and the original questions the leanest published configuration took 2.0 turns and 134k tokens, the heaviest 7.0 turns and 480k tokens.
          • With no index, one agent used 294k tokens on the light class and 261k tokens on the mid class on the original questions; the answers were 99% and 97% correct. A larger model class did not need far fewer tokens for the same lookups.

          Held-out questions

          Of the 6 configurations measured on both question sets, "PGR: code graph" and "PGR: graph and search" were fully correct in every run. On the held-out questions "PGR: graph and search" needed 181k tokens and 2.7 turns on the light class and 180k and 2.7 on the mid class; "Our engine, first generation, with embeddings" needed 134k tokens and 2.0 turns on the light class and 272k and 4.0 on the mid class.

          • Best, light class181k tokens, 2.7 turns, 3 of 3 fully correct
          • Best, mid class180k tokens, 2.7 turns, 3 of 3 fully correct
          • First generation, light class134k tokens, 2.0 turns, 2 of 3 fully correct
          • First generation, mid class272k tokens, 4.0 turns, 2 of 2 fully correct

          Parts

          Across both question sets, search alone listed every caller of a function in 1 of 6 light-class runs and 5 of 6 mid-class runs; graph alone did in 6 of 6 and 6 of 6. Graph alone took more turns on the light class: 4.0 on the original questions and 3.7 on the held-out questions, against 2.0 and 2.7 for graph and search together.

          Memory

          Memory that already held the answers, filled by a session that answered the same questions, matched the two-turn floor and did not beat it: "PGR: memory" and "PGR: graph, search and memory" took 2 turns on both classes and used 0.5k to 0.9k more tokens than graph and search without memory. Memory filled by an earlier session that explored the repository without seeing any question, compared cell by cell with graph and search without memory, used fewer tokens in 1 of 4 cells and more in 3 of 4; it was fully correct in 11 of 12 runs, against 12 of 12 without memory. These single lookups are not the task memory is for, and tasks that span sessions were not measured.

          • Exploring-session memory, fewer tokens than without memory1 of 4 cells
          • Exploring-session memory, more tokens than without memory3 of 4 cells
          • Exploring-session memory, fully correct runs11 of 12
          • Graph and search without memory, fully correct runs12 of 12

          Speed

          A query took 40 ms with "PGR" and 1937 ms with "Our engine, first generation", 48 times as long. Building the index took 0.52 s against 130 s, and the index is 8.1 MB against 14.5 MB. Query times were measured on one machine on the same day; the first generation's build time was measured in an earlier session.

          • PGR, query time40 ms
          • Our engine, first generation, query time1937 ms
          • PGR, build time0.52 s
          • Our engine, first generation, build time130 s
          • PGR, index size8.1 MB
          • Our engine, first generation, index size14.5 MB

          Orchestration

          With a reviewing orchestrator on top of three light workers, 48 of 48 runs ended fully correct, against 98 of 106 for one light agent alone; a run cost about 95 times as much.

          • Fully correct, orchestrated48 of 48
          • Fully correct, one light agent98 of 106
          • Cost of one runabout 95 times

          Tool hooks

          Tool hooks that compress and redact reads did not lower token use on these lookups, and without an index fewer runs ended fully correct (5 of 9 runs with the hooks on, 13 of 17 with them off).

          • Fully correct, hooks on5 of 9
          • Fully correct, hooks off13 of 17
